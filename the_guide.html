Submarineland

A guide to landing in southeastern Connecticut and living well — the geography, the work, the food, and the laws that will surprise you.

Southeastern Connecticut For Newcomers Updated October 20, 2026

Moving to southeastern Connecticut for Electric Boat, the submarine base, a design contract, or a transfer means getting to know a region shaped by the Thames River and the sea. Shipyards, military facilities, historic neighborhoods, and waterfront restaurants sit close together here. This guide is an introduction to the place around the work.

This region rewards curiosity. It has its own character, shaped by maritime industry, the Navy, research, and coastal communities. The following neighborhood impressions are editorial suggestions, not measured rankings. Use the guide to build a shortlist, then visit the places that interest you.

This full guide covers geography, neighborhoods, work, food, recreation, and practical legal considerations. Facts and source links were reviewed on October 20, 2026. Hiring plans, services, opening hours, and regulations can change; dated plans are identified as plans. The Ground Rules section is an orientation, not legal advice.

I

The Lay of the Land — What You're Actually Looking At

The Thames separates New London on the west bank from Groton on the east near its mouth. The Gold Star Memorial Bridge carries I-95 across the river. Electric Boat has its Groton shipyard and offices in New London; Naval Submarine Base New London is in Groton despite its name. The Thames Club is at 290 State Street in New London. Your specific worksite matters when choosing which side of the river to live on.

The east-west split is useful local knowledge. A home close to work on a map may still require a river crossing, parking, and a walk or shuttle to the job. Drive prospective routes at your actual shift time, and check current roadwork and traffic rather than relying on a single off-peak estimate.

Radiating outward, the region unfolds in distinct layers. Immediately south, the Thames meets Long Island Sound. To the east, the shoreline runs through Mystic, Noank, and Stonington before crossing into Rhode Island. To the west, Waterford and East Lyme lead toward Old Lyme and the Connecticut River valley. Each of these places has a personality. Together they form what the tourism boards call "Mystic Country" — a name that undersells it considerably.

New London Union Station provides Amtrak Northeast Regional service, including direct trains to New York and Boston. Journey times and stops vary by train. Check the current timetable for your date rather than assuming that Acela serves New London or that every journey takes the same time. Source: Amtrak station and booking information.

II

Where to Live — A Candid Tour of Your Options

Housing costs vary by address, property type, condition, and the date of the listing. Compare current rentals and recent comparable sales rather than treating an undated regional average as a budget. Include taxes, insurance, heating, parking, and commuting costs. Tour the property and confirm the lease or sale terms before committing.

Renting can be a useful way to learn the region before buying, but the right decision depends on your circumstances. Search current listings and local property managers, compare what is included in the rent, and confirm whether a lease is year-round or seasonal. Ask about deposits, pets, utilities, and renewal terms. Do not assume that a village’s reputation predicts the price or quality of every available home.

Rural Settings · Larger Parcels · Forest Access
North Stonington / Voluntown

North Stonington and Voluntown offer rural alternatives to the shoreline centers. Pachaug State Forest, Connecticut’s largest state forest, extends across six towns and provides trails and other outdoor recreation. Acreage can bring additional responsibilities: inspect wells, septic systems, access roads, and maintenance needs. Commute time and route vary substantially by address; Route 2 is not a universal direct route to the submarine base. Test the journey and confirm internet service before committing to a home.

Young engineers come from across the country to work at Electric Boat. Then they discover the coast. The question changes from where to work to where to stay.

III

The Work — What Electric Boat Actually Is Right Now

Electric Boat’s regional role is substantial: it designs and builds submarines, and its work supports both the Virginia-class attack-submarine and Columbia-class ballistic-missile-submarine programs. These are distinct programs with different missions, contracts, and schedules.

On July 29, 2026, General Dynamics announced awards to Electric Boat for five additional Columbia-class submarines and nine additional Virginia-class submarines, alongside infrastructure support. The announced construction awards were $29.5 billion and $42.1 billion respectively. Those contract amounts are not simple per-boat retail prices or the total lifetime cost of either program. Source: General Dynamics, July 29, 2026.

Electric Boat’s Waterford campus announcement describes plans to hire 8,000 people in 2026 and another 8,000 in 2027. These are hiring plans, not confirmed completed hires or equivalent increases in net headcount. The company’s public overview describes a workforce exceeding 23,000; it does not provide a dated October headcount. Source: EB Careers; company overview.

For a newcomer, the useful questions are specific to the offer: Which site and shift will you work? What training is included? What overtime is expected? Where will you park, and how long is the walk or shuttle ride to the work area? Ask the hiring team to explain these before comparing housing options.

If the position requires a security clearance or other access authorization, ask which conditions must be satisfied before you can start and who will communicate the decision. Processing time varies. A general relocation guide cannot promise a start date, predict a clearance outcome, or guarantee a particular pace of promotion.

IV

Eating & Drinking — What the Region Does Exceptionally Well

Southeastern Connecticut has a food culture built on a foundation of exceptional seafood and sustained by the steady influx of educated, curious professionals who want more than chain restaurants. The baseline — fresh lobster, oysters, steamers, fish and chips, chowder — is among the best in the country. The ceiling is higher than most newcomers expect.

This selection includes restaurants in Mystic, Noank, Stonington, New London, and Groton. It is a starting list rather than a comprehensive ranking. Waterfront seafood, fresh pasta, Thai food, and more formal dining offer different reasons to explore beyond your daily commute.

    Chef David Standridge's restaurant at The Whaler's Inn emphasizes sustainably sourced seafood and seasonal New England ingredients, including lesser-used local species.     Waterfront seafood and Connecticut-style hot lobster rolls — warm lobster with butter. Abbott's serves soft drinks and welcomes guests to bring their own beer or wine. Cash and major credit and debit cards are accepted. Check its published season calendar before making the trip; do not assume it closes on Labor Day.

  • Captain Scott's Lobster Dock
    New London · 80 Hamilton Street · Shaw’s Cove · Seasonal

    A seasonal waterfront seafood destination at Shaw's Cove, serving lobster rolls and New England seafood. The restaurant takes its name from mariner and salvager Captain Thomas A. Scott. Check its current opening dates before visiting: regular service is seasonal, with occasional holiday pop-ups announced separately.

V

Getting Out — What to Do When You're Not Working

The outdoors here is excellent and underused by newcomers who haven't yet learned to read the calendar. The key insight: southeastern Connecticut is a three-season destination that behaves like a year-round one if you're willing to dress for it and if you know where the hunting, fishing, and wildlife laws apply — which they do, actively and specifically. Section VII covers what you need to know before you put a line in the water or a bow in your hands.

Long Island Sound and the Thames offer opportunities for boating, with conditions that demand attention to tides, weather, commercial traffic, and your experience. Use suitable charts and follow applicable boating rules. Block Island Express operates from New London; confirm its seasonal schedule, crossing time, and return sailing before planning a day trip. Source: Block Island Express.

Mystic Seaport Museum explores maritime history through vessels, exhibits, and its historic waterfront. Its collection includes the Charles W. Morgan, the surviving wooden whaleship. The Submarine Force Museum and USS Nautilus in Groton offer another perspective on maritime technology. Museum admission and parking are free; confirm current opening days and vessel access before traveling. Mystic Seaport Museum; Submarine Force Museum visitor information.

USS Nautilus was the world’s first nuclear-powered submarine. A visit can help newcomers connect the region’s submarine work with its history. Check the museum’s current notices for closures or restrictions before making a special trip.

Fort Griswold Battlefield State Park preserves a Revolutionary War battlefield in Groton. Check current park information for monument and museum access before visiting. Park information.

The Garde Arts Center at 325 State Street in New London presents film, music, theater, and other performances. Consult its calendar for the current program. Foxwoods and Mohegan Sun provide additional entertainment inland; travel times depend on your starting point and traffic. For Sailfest and other waterfront events, confirm dates and programming with the organizer before planning around them. Garde events.

Foxwoods and Mohegan Sun are separate resort destinations. Compare the specific show, restaurant, travel arrangements, and total cost before choosing a visit.

For waterfront festivals, check the organizer’s current schedule, parking arrangements, and any weather-related changes. Annual events do not guarantee identical dates or programming each year.

VI

Things Nobody Tells You — The Honest Version

Test the commute. The Gold Star Bridge, approaches to the shipyard and base, roadwork, and shift-change traffic can affect the trip. Include parking and the walk to your work area. A short map distance does not guarantee a short journey.

Plan for winter. Snow, freezing rain, and cold weather can affect roads and household costs. Check your vehicle’s tires and maintenance, and adjust travel to conditions. Ask for a property’s prior energy bills and the heating-system details; there is no single reliable monthly utility estimate for every home.

Mystic is not a town. It is a village that spans the border of Groton and Stonington, has no independent municipal government, and will confound anyone trying to look up its school district, zip code, or tax rate. Half of it is in the Groton school system; the other half is Stonington. Know which side of the river you're on before you make a real estate decision.

Connecticut's alcohol retail rules are unusual. Grocery stores sell beer only — not wine, not spirits. For wine or liquor you go to a package store. Package stores are open Sundays from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. (a change made in 2012; older guides may say otherwise), Monday–Saturday 8 a.m. to 10 p.m., and closed on Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year's Day. Bars and restaurants serve seven days a week. The system has its quirks but it works.

The ferry is underused by newcomers. Cross Sound Ferry runs from New London to Orient Point (Long Island). Block Island Ferry runs from New London as well. This is a legitimate way to spend a Saturday and most new arrivals don't discover it for months.

Compare the full tax picture. Connecticut income-tax liability depends on taxable income, filing status, credits, and other rules. Property-tax bills depend on assessed value and applicable local rates, including additional districts where relevant. Obtain the current bill for a specific property and consult the Department of Revenue Services for current income-tax guidance. A lower purchase price alone does not establish a lower total cost.

Give yourself time to know the place. Visit the same neighborhoods in different weather and at different times of day. Explore local institutions, markets, performances, and clubs. Returning to places you enjoy is a practical way to build connections and develop your own view of the region.

Connecticut law will surprise you. The next section is not supplementary reading. It contains information that will affect you within the first week if you own firearms, fish, hunt, or simply drive. Read it before you unpack.

VII

The Ground Rules — What Connecticut Expects of You Legally

Legal guidance reviewed October 20, 2026. Moving here can change your licensing, storage, driving, and outdoor-recreation obligations. This is a practical orientation, not legal advice or a complete statement of every exception. Use the official sources below for your circumstances; consult a Connecticut attorney about a specific legal problem.

Firearms — Check Before You Move

An out-of-state carry permit alone does not authorize ordinary handgun carry in Connecticut. Resident applicants generally start with their local issuing authority for a temporary permit and then apply for the state permit. Nonresidents use DESPP's separate application process. Applicants must be at least 21 and meet the statutory eligibility and training requirements; a permit is not guaranteed merely because you completed a course.

For applications subject to the requirements effective July 1, 2024, the approved safety course must have been completed within the preceding two years and include the required Connecticut-law instruction and live fire. Check the application instructions before paying for training.

Check each firearm and magazine before bringing it into the state. Connecticut regulates defined assault weapons, large-capacity magazines, and certain unserialized firearms. Definitions, exemptions, and deadlines matter, including provisions affecting some military personnel. Do not assume that an item legal in your former state is legal here, or that a pistol permit resolves possession restrictions.

Safe storage is not limited to homes with children. Section 29-37i requires a firearm kept on premises under your control to be secured as specified by law, unless carried on your person or close enough for ready retrieval and use. The statute allows a securely locked container or another manner a reasonable person would believe secure; it does not make a trigger lock an automatic answer in every circumstance.

A carry permit does not override location restrictions. State-park and forest recreation and camping rules restrict weapons except where specifically authorized; designated hunting areas and authorized ranges have their own rules. Check the specific property and activity before taking a firearm onto state land.

Sources: DESPP permit FAQ; DESPP firearms guidance; Chapter 529, including §§ 29-28, 29-35 and 29-37i; DEEP recreation and camping rules.

Self-Defense — The Limits Matter

Connecticut's self-defense statute permits reasonably necessary force against what a person reasonably believes to be the use or imminent use of physical force. Deadly force has a higher threshold: a reasonable belief that the other person is using or about to use deadly force, or inflicting or about to inflict great bodily harm.

A person generally cannot use deadly force if they know they can avoid its necessity with complete safety by retreating. The dwelling or workplace exception applies when the person was not the initial aggressor. Being at home does not remove the other requirements for justified force. Provocation, initial aggression, and other statutory restrictions can defeat a claim of self-defense; defense of premises is separately addressed. Do not treat a warning shot as a legally harmless alternative.

Source: Connecticut General Statutes §§ 53a-19 and 53a-20.

Driving & Moving to Connecticut

Most new residents have 90 days to transfer their driver's license and vehicle registration. DMV identifies exceptions, including qualifying active-duty military personnel and their dependents, and full-time students maintaining permanent residence elsewhere. Check your status instead of assuming that every military-connected employee is exempt.

Handheld phone use while driving is prohibited, including temporary stops in traffic. Teen drivers face additional restrictions on hands-free use, with limited statutory exceptions. Published distracted-driving fines are $200 for a first offense, $375 for a second, and $625 for subsequent offenses.

When approaching covered roadside vehicles, slow down and move over when it is safe; do not make an unsafe lane change. Stop for a school bus's flashing red lights as required. The opposite-direction exception for a bus on a separate roadway of a divided highway does not turn an ordinary painted centerline into an exemption.

Sources: DMV new-resident requirements and exceptions; vehicle registration; distracted driving; DMV driver's manual.

Impaired Driving — Court and DMV Consequences

Connecticut uses DUI, OUI, and DWI terminology; the important issue is impaired operation. Alcohol, drugs, or a combination can support an impaired-driving charge. A driver can be prosecuted for impairment even without a test at or above a statutory alcohol threshold.

Criminal proceedings and DMV administrative action are separate. A qualifying failed or refused chemical test generally triggers a 45-day administrative suspension, followed by ignition-interlock requirements. Interlock duration depends on age, history, and whether the test was failed or refused. A conviction can carry additional criminal and licensing consequences. Refusing a test does not avoid consequences, and it is inaccurate to describe every refusal as a longer initial suspension. Get legal advice promptly about an actual arrest and its deadlines.

Source: DMV impaired-driving and ignition-interlock guidance.

Alcohol & Hosting

Connecticut permits happy-hour promotions, subject to its liquor rules. Promotions must apply equally to patrons; bottomless or all-you-can-drink promotions are prohibited. Service hours depend on permit type and applicable local restrictions, and businesses may close earlier.

Adults hosting gatherings must take underage possession seriously. The social-host law addresses knowingly, recklessly, or with criminal negligence permitting illegal possession by a minor on premises under one's control, and knowingly failing to make reasonable efforts to stop it. Furnishing alcohol is separately regulated. Narrow statutory exceptions exist, including specified family circumstances; they are not a general permission to serve other people's children. Cannabis is also covered by social-host provisions. Do not rely on a guide's simplified penalty label to decide whether conduct is lawful.

Sources: DCP restaurant liquor guide; §§ 30-86, 30-89 and 30-89a.

Fishing & Recreational Lobstering

Anglers age 16 and older generally need the appropriate inland or marine license; combination licenses and exemptions exist. Some inland fishing also requires a trout and salmon stamp. Marine reciprocity with Maine, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, and New York has conditions: Connecticut residents must hold a Connecticut marine license, and marine reciprocity does not cover inland fishing.

DEEP's 2026 recreational striped-bass rule permits one fish daily, at least 28 inches but less than 31 inches. Inline circle hooks are required when fishing for striped bass with bait, subject to DEEP's stated exceptions. Other species have their own seasons, sizes, possession limits, and prohibitions. Check the current species table before each trip; do not treat a short list here as a complete fishing regulation book.

Recreational lobstering requires the appropriate personal-use license and compliance with gear, season, and possession rules. DEEP's personal-use guidance lists a carapace range of 3⅜ to 5¼ inches, not a 4¾-inch minimum. Egg-bearing and V-notched lobsters have protection requirements. Review the current instructions before setting gear or keeping a catch.

Sources: licenses and exemptions; marine reciprocity; current marine species regulations; personal-use lobster guidelines.

Hunting — Read the Current Online Rules

Connecticut recognizes qualifying prior resident hunting licenses in the relevant discipline from the preceding five years, or approved hunter education, including recognized equivalent out-of-state training. Experienced newcomers are not automatically required to repeat Connecticut's course. The documentation and training requirements depend on the license and activity.

Sunday hunting is permitted on qualifying private land for authorized species and methods during open seasons; it is not a general opening of public land. Sunday migratory-bird hunting remains prohibited. The Sunday restriction near a blazed public trail is 40 yards; DEEP warns that the printed 2026 guide incorrectly says 40 feet. Deer and turkey hunting require written landowner permission. Effective October 1, 2026, verbal landowner permission is sufficient for small-game hunting, including Sundays.

Fluorescent-orange requirements, species permits, harvest reporting, bag limits, and legal implements vary by activity and season. Use the online guide and the rules for the specific hunting area before going out.

Sources: DEEP license qualifications; 2026 hunting laws, corrections, and permission rules.

Protected Wildlife

Many native birds are protected by federal law, with additional protections for eagles and listed species. Taking birds, nests, eggs, or feathers can require authorization; specific exceptions and permit rules matter. Observe wildlife without handling it or disturbing active nests. Connecticut's timber rattlesnake is protected: do not kill or collect one.

For injured or distressed wildlife, seek instructions from DEEP's 24-hour dispatch: 860-424-3333. Keep people and pets back and follow the responding agency's directions.

Sources: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service; DEEP timber rattlesnake guidance.

Cannabis — Employment, Military Service & Clearances

Connecticut allows adult-use cannabis for people 21 and older within state-law limits. That does not create unrestricted permission to possess or use it at work, on military installations, or in a safety-sensitive role. Employer policies, military rules, and federal security requirements must be checked separately.

Do not assume that every federal contractor employee is subject to an identical testing requirement, or that any use automatically revokes a clearance. National-security adjudication considers the person's circumstances under the applicable guidelines. Employees and applicants should consult their employer's written policy and facility security officer about current obligations, reporting, and testing before relying on state legalization.

Sources: Connecticut workplace guidance; Security Executive Agent's marijuana adjudication guidance.
